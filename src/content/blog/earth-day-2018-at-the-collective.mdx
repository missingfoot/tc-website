Earth Day is currently the largest environmental movement of the world, celebrated by more than a billion people every year. A single day of action that changes human behaviour and provokes policy changes, Earth Day started back in 1970. This year, The Collective joined in.

This year’s theme spotlighted an incredibly important subject: plastic pollution. Thousands of national and local governments, organisations, faith groups and corporations adopted Earth Day Network’s ‘End Plastic Pollution’ campaign and acted to reduce their plastic footprints. It was no different in the UK, where a ban on plastic straws and cotton buds was proposed. The prime minister said plastic waste is “one of the greatest environmental challenges facing the world.”

The idea for a national day to recognise the environment came to the founder of Earth Day Gaylord Nelson, a former United States senator from Wisconsin, after witnessing the ravages of the 1969 massive oil spill in Santa Barbara, California. On April 22, 1970, 20 million Americans took to the streets, parks, and auditoriums to demonstrate for a healthy, sustainable environment in massive coast-to-coast rallies that took over the whole country.

For Earth Day 2018 the topic was **Ending Plastic Pollution**. Now more than ever, people around the world are hungry to engage in important environmental issues. **Communities and businesses must take the lead promoting action to end plastic pollution**, especially the impact caused by single-use plastic.

The Collective is focused on **creating structures to help our members to be more aware and understand the impact that plastic pollution** has on their everyday lives, and for our community to know how simply we can make a massive impact on this problem, through minor behavioural changes. That is why we joined Earth Day 2018 this year, to show the world that **we** **care when it comes to the environmental impact of ending plastic pollution**.

That’s why we hosted an all day festival on the **22nd of April** to show our commitment to co-creating a more positive environmental impact at [The Collective Old Oak](/locations/old-oak), the biggest co-living building in the world.

We had many activities throughout the day, starting with a presentation about the history of Earth Day and how it connects with the Impact team’s objectives for The Collective Old Oak. It was interesting to see what changed regarding societal environmental impact since 1970, when the movement was created, until now – we’ve come a long way to protect the earth through important legislation such as the Clean Air Act and more recently, the Paris Agreement but there certainly is a long way to go. <br /> <br /> Then we had a Brunch Quiz, with environmental facts about plastic pollution and climate change. It was interesting to observe that many of us still aren’t aware the current state of this issue. Our member Luigi Meschini remarked: “The games during brunch were really useful to learn how the situation really is.”

After that, we went for a Canal Walk to help clean up our neighbourhood. Getting started in our own surroundings was particularly gratifying on such a beautiful day. In fact, our member Mansour Ahmed said: “I had never walked alongside the canal since I came to live at The Collective Old Oak,” and noted that it’s something he plans to do more often now. A squad of ten adventurous individuals braved it amongst the piles of rubbish clogging the margins and the waters of our nearby Grand Union Canal. We were greeted by pedestrians and boat owners, and some even promised to join next time. We came back home with heavy bags full of rubbish, but our conscience was definitely lighter after having actively done our part for our canal.<br /> <br /> After the canal clean-up, we had very interesting talks about plastic pollution on our local community with Cecilia from HEART (Harlesden Environmental Action Residents Team). We also explored the potential to have a more sustainable approach to our living standards in The Collective Old Oak in our discussion about the Sustainable Households Survey Findings from the [RSA](https://www.thersa.org/) (Royal Society for the encouragement of Arts, Manufactures and Commerce), which was presented by RSA fellows Bryonie Badcock, Tanuja Pandit and Anna Pitt. <br /> <br /> Our last discussion was focusing on the individual level about how important it is to be intentional about our lifestyle and behaviours, and how much small changes can lead to big results. It represented a call to action, as we didn’t want to go out of this event only with words, and concepts on our heads, but also with some ideas to use firsthand. We had a discussion session on living with zero waste with Joanna Bucur, our former Community Manager at The Collective Old Oak. Florentine Malot from Unpackaged also joined us to enrich the discussion with facts and a business perspective on reducing our environmental impact. <br /> <br /> On our break, we all went to check out the sculpture from the local artist Stuart Mccaffer, entitled Everywhere Else, pictured above. It’s a very interesting piece of art and relevant as well as it’s made from plastic waste to represent the anthropogenic impact on our planet.

Our last activity was screening of documentary, [A Plastic Ocean](https://plasticoceans.org/about-film/). In this adventure documentary, journalist Craig Leeson teams up with free diver Tanya Streeter and an international team of scientists and researchers. They travel to twenty locations around the world over four years to explore the fragile state of our oceans, uncovering ugly truths about plastic pollution, and revealing working solutions that can be put into immediate effect. It was very alarming for us to see the magnitude of the impact that plastic pollution has on our planet. <br />

Our Earth Day festival generated awareness amongst our members as well as a potential for agency, as many participants shared how this awareness has inspired them to have a different attitude towards plastic pollution. Our member Bryonie Badcock said: “My reusable coffee cup is back in my bag, and ready to be used.” Another member Anna Grace Gallagher said: “I am now more conscious about daily living items I am using. I think more about the things I actually need.” <br />

Other ripple effects that came out from our Earth Day initiative was that our business partners Energym and Atterstalla Cafe are going plastic free. Our members will also self-organise to send a petition to Amazon to ask them to reduce their packaging.

It’s amazing what an intentional Sunday can do, no?
