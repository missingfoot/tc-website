More than 500 different people live at The Collective Old Oak, together creating a rich community full of different interests, skills and experiences. Take Devany and Graham, who hail from Canada and bring their love of culture, travel and connection to the community. Since becoming members, they have both become key pillars of the community, getting involved in attending and organising events for other members – when they aren’t off adventuring, that is. Here they chat to friend and member Aden about memories, love, and Greek pigeons.

<strong>Aden: What is the most exciting aspect of living in London as a couple?<br /></strong> Graham: Well Devany is my personal travel and itinerary person. She finds all sorts of cool things for us to do that we wouldn’t be able to find in a small city. Often many of them a free. The most exciting aspect for us would be going to visit lots of museums and shows. Also being able to connect with like-minded people, which is often hard to find in a small city.

Devany: With all the cool, fun and interesting things that we get to try, I get to see it through my own eyes but I also get to experience and appreciate it through Graham’s eyes. I like to see how he appreciates it and the things that he pulls out of the experiences. I notice this the most when we went to art galleries, in which we would look at the same painting but each of us would have different feeling and impressions.

**Aden: You love travel, what’s the funniest thing that ever happened to you on vacation**?<br /> Devany: We were in Greece, which was beautiful. To this day we still don’t have an understanding as to why the Greek people find pigeons so special. We realised that if we opened our umbrellas, all the pigeons do this one-foot hop and they sit back down on the ground. I thought it was cool because I thought Graham was giving them a force-push, like in *Star Wars*. So we went to make a video of Graham force-pushing (disclaimer: no pigeons were harmed) and as expected, the pigeons did a one-foot hop. However, an elderly Greek woman found this action rude and walked up to Graham, in a middle of public park, in front of the museums and abruptly slapped him. She proceeded to scream at him in Greek and chased us out of the park because he was accosting the pigeons. She continued to chase us down the street and flipped us off. This was a very interesting cultural experience.

<strong>Aden: What’s the most precious childhood memory?<br /></strong> Devany: My most precious childhood memory? I would say spending time with my great-grandma, her name was Julia. She passed away at 97 years old when I was 20 years old. She was just wonderful throughout my whole life. We were both the black sheep of the family, both a little eccentric, artistic and passionate to try different things. That wasn’t the vibe of the rest of our family. It was wonderful to have a multi-generational support for my creative side.

Graham: We had a beach close to our house were I grew up. My brother and I would go down the beach together, bare foot. We had to make sure that we didn’t cut up our feet from the barnacles but we figured if we built up our feet, after a while, it will be OK. We built rafts from driftwood, bulk-kelp and would sit and watch the sunset. It was very peaceful. A peaceful place to grow up and see nature.

<strong>Aden: What’s the most interesting experience you had living at The Collective?<br /></strong> Graham: The Collective had an event, where there was a microbiologist professor, who gave a talk on bioluminescent bacteria. He sort of married both science and art and gave a compelling presentation. We had tonic drinks that glowed under a black light. That was very cool.

<strong>Aden: You host a a Bible study group at The Collective, what inspired you to start that?<br /></strong> Devany: We are passionate about studying the Bible and what it teaches, and we attended Bible study back in Canada and loved it. Since The Collective is about fostering community and relationships, we thought that Bible study would be a wonderful addition to this wonderful and flourishing community.

<strong>Aden: What do you love most about each other?<br /></strong> Devany: He’s sacrificial love for me. He is so kind and absolutely willing to give up anything in the world to help me be a better person; enjoy life more and our marriage. He’s devotion I would say is what I love the most about my husband.

Graham: On the same lines but more on helping to the nth degree. Everything that she does, she does with great fervour and intensity. If she’s going to research something, she’s research it for hours. If she’s going to work on something, she’ll work very hard. So I would say her conviction is what I love most about my wife.
