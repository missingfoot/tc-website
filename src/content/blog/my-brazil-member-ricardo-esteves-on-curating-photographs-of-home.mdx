As a portraitist, Ricardo Esteves is used to working with subjects to bring their uniqueness forward. As the mentor of eight rising Brazilian photographers, he does much the same. “I’m helping them to access their inner skills and feelings,” he says of the project, which is culminating in an exhibition opening in Paris today.

*My Brazil* explores themes of home and identity, showcasing creatives’ unique perspectives on their country of origin. Marco Escada, Luis Todeschi, Marle eReinaldo, Igor Gomes, Rose, Aguiar, Guilherme Costa Pinto, Adrien Luke, Maria Elizabeth Broxado worked with Ricardo over a three month period, participating in weekly sessions where they worked to portray Brazil through their individual aesthetics.

That the exhibition takes place in Paris makes sense for Ricardo, who has spent the past ten years working between this city, and London. From his current West London home at The Collective Old Oak, Ricardo reflected on the project and shared an insight into what he’s up to next.

<strong>So Ricardo, you have been busily curating this exhibition, which opens today. What prompted it?</strong>

Ricardo: In 2015, when I did my eighth art exhibition in France, I realised that I had a significant professional experience after 22 years working as an art photographer and that this skills could be shared with other artists. I can help make their dream possible, because they were undergoing the same problems as I did when I was starting out.

<strong>What drew you to the photographers you decided on for the exhibition?</strong>

Their differences. Brazil is a country the size of Europe, and each one of these photographers sees their country in a different way. The theme of the exhibition, ‘My Brazil’, is Brazil from their point of view, and I believe this diversity will be interesting for Europeans visiting the exhibition. As a portraitist I’m used to searching inside people to bring a trait of their personality to the surface. I’m essentially doing the same with the photographers I’m mentoring, helping them to access their inner skills and feelings.

<strong>Can you describe the process involved in curating the show?</strong>

With this exhibition, I’m involved from the beginning: defining with them their artistic photographic project and what they want to achieve in the next five years. I use with them coaching tools to accelerate the art process. The aim is to make them understand what they really want and what are the actions to be taken to reach their goal.

The main focus of this kind of mentoring is to discover where is the uniqueness of an artist, understand what is their DNA, their identity, and incorporate it into their work. When they reach this point any subject they treat will have the same signature along their career. One month before the exhibition, myself and Anna Priscilla Marques, who is a culture project developer in Brazil, came together to collaborate on the curating process for the exhibition.

<strong>What were the biggest challenges you faced in curating the exhibition?</strong>

The greatest challenge for the curatorship in this exhibition is precisely to connect the artistic diversity in the space of the gallery and try to establish a connection between the eight artists. But, there are also everyday problems that are difficult to handle, like the train strike that will happen in France the day of the exhibition!

<strong>You are a photographer yourself – what are the main themes behind your work?</strong>

I am very interested in social phenomena and I like to interact with the human being in my photographs. Curiously, all of my artistic work and my creative process focusses on identity – of people, groups, cities, and events.

<strong>You are currently working on a photographic project involving the Brazilian community in Brent, can you tell us a little more about this?</strong>

The OPDC (Old Oak and Park Royal Development Corporation) is interested in exploring and communicating the development of the large Brazilian community that lives in the Brent area. But they discovered that this community is quite isolated, and that many of its members do not speak English. This was the starting point of our project: discovering how we could make a difference and improve social integration between those people and the rest of the community. For the project, I’m taking photos and my wife, Lucilia, is interviewing each subject.

<strong>How big will the project be and where do you see it going?</strong>

I want to have around 50 photo shots of a variety of Brazilians from this community. The could lead to an exhibition, a book, or some other project – wait and see!
