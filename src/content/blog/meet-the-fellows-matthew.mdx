Right now at The Collective Old Oak, six fellows are taking part in our first ever Community Facilitator Programme. That means they’re on the ground for the next few months, supporting our members in making their home amazing. Along the way, they’re on a peer-led journey exploring tools to foster a brilliant community experience.

We’re spotlighting each fellow in a series of profiles. Today, meet Matt!

<Figure src="/images/blog/meet-the-fellows-matthew/cover.webp" alt="the collective old oak west london co-living community" caption="“I hope that the meaning and feeling of unconditional love can be shared by all.”" />

Matthew Lesniak is a French-American urban analyst. His work has connected him with grassroots initiatives, nonprofit organisations and public authorities from around the world — from France to New Zealand, from Peru to Kenya — on topics spanning urban agriculture, post-disaster temporary urbanism, sharing economy and place-making.

On participating in the Facilitator programme, Matt said: “I see this opportunity as a social experiment, both personally and for The Collective, and I am excited to learn new techniques for curating ideas, community, and personal and transformational growth from The Collective team, my fellowship peers and residents.”

Matthew told us more about himself by finishing some sentences:

<strong><em>To me community is…</em></strong> <br /> “A space where people feel comfortable to share and be vulnerable with one another, where trust and respect are unspoken values, and where honesty and authenticity are the golden rules. Community can be in a physical space or an online space, but the importance of physical interactions and face-to-face support is invaluable. A community works together to encourage and support each individual in that space to thrive and be empowered, but also works as a whole to contribute positively to the human condition and our planet.”

<strong><em>It’s important to connect with people because…</em></strong> <br /> “As an individual we never have all the answers! There are strengths in numbers because everyone can bring their own knowledge, talents and expertise to a particular situation or exchange, and connecting with others can lead to solutions that we as individuals may have never thought of.”

<strong><em>My biggest fear is…</em></strong> <br /> “That my doubts will prohibit me from reaching my full potential.”

<strong><em>I am happiest when…</em> <br /> “</strong>I am in nature. When I am nature I feel a sense of purity and clarity that reminds me that we are not just living on this planet, but that the chemical compositions and the atoms in our bodies come from ancient star explosions that make it so that the universe exists within us — just as much as we are apart of the universe. Astrophysicist Neil Degrasse Tyson [says it best](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9D05ej8u-gU).”

<strong><em>In the future, I hope…</em><br /></strong> “That the media will cease to be run by fear, that politicians will respect the needs of their constituents more than the renewal of their terms, and that social, cultural and natural capital becomes more valuable to people than economic capital. More importantly, I hope that the meaning and feeling of unconditional love can be shared by all.”
