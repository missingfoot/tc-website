Raghad is a member of [The Collective](/) and a passionate campaigner for humanitarian issues, working tirelessly to improve rights for migrants and refugees. In light of [Refugee Week](http://refugeeweek.org.uk/), a programme of arts, cultural and educational events and activities that celebrates the contribution of refugees to the UK, Raghad reflects on her journey with refugee activism and her most recent project, ‘Live As One’, held at [The Collective Old Oak:](/locations/old-oak)

From a very young age, I wanted to change politics to make it more sensitive to humanitarian issues. I knew that in order to change the system, I needed to understand it first. So I did my undergraduate degree in Politics and International Relations – to understand the system – followed by a Masters in Public Policy – to change the system.

Since October 2016, I have been part of [Oxfam’s Constituency Campaigner Program,](https://www.oxfam.org.uk/get-involved/campaign-with-us/how-to-campaign/constituency-campaigner-programme) representing Oxfam’s aims and campaigns through influencing local MPs to take political/humanitarian actions regarding various local and global issues. However, my focus was mainly on their [‘Stand As One’](https://www.oxfam.org.uk/get-involved/campaign-with-us/conflict-and-emergencies/stand-as-one) campaign, which works on supporting refugees and making sure that we rebuild their torn-apart lives.

As part of this, I have been emailing MP’s promoting the Refugees Family Reunion bill. Together we have helped make a huge step forward, with MPs voting to support the bill that, if successful, will help keep families together.

> In 2017, I moved to London from Southampton. The transition from a small town to a big city was exciting, but very challenging. I remember walking around London thinking that regardless of how busy this place is – diverse and full of beautiful buildings – sometimes it still feels too small, making it hard to breathe. Are Londoners just as beautiful as those buildings from the outside, but so empty from within? Are we feeling that alienated?

A few months passed and I got to know the people who also live in my building [The Collective Old Oak,](/locations/old-oak) and that’s when everything changed. There is an Arabic saying that says: “Even heaven wouldn’t be enjoyed if it had no people.” In meeting this community, I finally understood these words. A community can be defined as a group of people living in the same place or having a particular characteristic in common. But The Collective offers a beautiful new meaning, as it welcomes people from all around the world and from different cultures, religions, personalities and from all walks of life. Here, they come together, share their energy and skills, and live collectively.

### Live As One 2018

This sense of a diverse community was a major factor in why I chose The Collective as the venue for my latest project, ‘Live As One’. Location also played a part: it felt significant to host the event in a borough that is home to large number of refugees and migrants. Held in late May, the event itself was a day of talks and educational moments. Overall, it was an opportunity to kick start future projects that would ensure the local community and change-makers in the area support and encourage refugees and migrants within the Ealing borough – and all over London.

Rosie, one of The Collective’s community hosts, excitedly came on board in the coordination. And the more we spoke about the forthcoming event, the more people were happy to help make it possible. Most of the volunteers were Collective members: Sam, Sabeeh, Matty Pepprell, and Serwan. With our joint effort and passion towards the issue, the event was amazingly organised and a big success.

As a message to all the readers, believers, dreamers and the future, I’d like to end this article with a few lines from my ‘Live As One’ speech:

“Maybe and only maybe, if we start listening to the life jackets on the shores of Greece and others, rather than recoding and sharing their statistics all over the media, maybe and only maybe then can we understand and relate to their backgrounds, stories, dreams and hopes, and to the things that makes them and all of us who we truly are. And maybe and only maybe then, we will realise the privilege we are all given to welcome every single refugee and migrant into Europe and all around the world.

Because every migrant and refugee is born with a dream, raised with hope and live with determination. Their love for life led them to the path of those who are usually seen as the future shapers. Those who might not be able to control the wind, but will always adjust their sail. So, cheers to those who collectively sail through life with love, support and hope. Your efforts are as radiant as the sun that warms us all through the journey’s tough times and as vibrant as the stars that guide us home. Thank you all for helping us discover the connected, unified and diverse world we envision ourselves living in. A world where we live as one!”
