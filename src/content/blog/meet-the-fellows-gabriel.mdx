Right now at The Collective Old Oak, six fellows are taking part in our first ever Community Facilitator Programme. That means they’re on the ground for the next few months, supporting our members in making their community amazing. Along the way, they’re on a peer-led journey exploring tools to foster a brilliant community experience.

We’re spotlighting our fellows in a series of profiles. Next up, meet Gabriel!

<Figure src="/images/blog/meet-the-fellows-gabriel/cover.webp" alt="the collective old oak co-living west london community" caption="“Connection is in our human essence.”" />

Growing up in a rural area of Rio de Janeiro, Gabriel spent his childhood climbing trees, bathing in rivers and camping in the mountains of the Atlantic jungle. These life experiences fostered in him a deep desire to mitigate negative human impact on the natural world.

Informed by studies in ecology and strategic leadership towards sustainability, he co-founded [Favela Verde](http://favelaverde.org/), an environmental and educational NGO in Favela da Rocinha, the biggest favela, or shanty-town, in Latin America. Using a bottom-up approach that is interactive, collective, experiential, dynamic and accessible, Favela Verde boosts creativity and opens generative social debates between residents.

Now, Gabriel is bringing his experience and passion to The Collective. He told us more about himself by finishing some sentences:

<strong>It’s important to connect with people because…<br /></strong> “We are the ‘ultimate social mammals.’ Connection is in our human essence. Our brain has been developed throughout evolution to respond and create strong bonds and meaningful connections, simply because we would not survive alone in prehistoric times. With urbanisation and the technical revolution, we isolated ourselves from others, as we could afford to survive alone because of the comfort technology provided us. I believe this isolation is the main cause of all the mental diseases and acts of violence we are experiencing now.”

<strong>My biggest fear is…</strong><br /> “Living my life without contributing to a better society — looking back in the moment of my final transition and not feeling proud of what I created.”

<strong>I am happiest when…</strong><br /> “I am in nature, connecting with my land in the Atlantic jungle.”

<strong>In the future, I hope…<br /> “</strong>To see our society embracing the diversity as an asset, rather than something to be afraid of. To see and co-create spaces for true connection amongst individuals, and ideas for a better world for all living beings.”
