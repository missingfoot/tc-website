Reading books is good for you, there’s no doubt about it. But reading in a group can be even more beneficial for your wellbeing and mental health. At [The Collective Old Oak](/co-living) we have a Book Club, and I find that being able to discuss what I’ve read, whether fiction or nonfiction, allows me to get a new perspective on my reading experience. I also find that when reading a book for a book club, I tend to pay more attention to what I’m reading, as I know we will have a conversation about it later on.

Recently with our book club, we’ve read Yuval Noah Harari’s *Sapiens: The History of Humankind* and Mark Manson’s *The Subtle Art of Not Giving a F\*ck*. Sapiens definitely created an interesting discussion and gave us a new perspective about life and the history of our species, while Mark Manson’s book opened up a discussion about our values and how to stop caring about things that are not worth our time and energy. Our upcoming books are always chosen by voting for the most popular option, and there is no harm no foul if you cannot attend one or more of our meetings.

With this in mind, I’ve outlined a few more reasons to give a book club a try.

<strong>You meet new people.</strong> Being a part of a book club exposes you to new people, opinions and ideas, an allows you to get to know your existing friends better. The more versatile the backgrounds of the book club members, the more different points of view for the books you’ll get!

> Being a part of a book club exposes you to new people, opinions and ideas, an allows you to get to know your existing friends better.

<strong>You get to widen your bookshelf.</strong> When in a book club, you might get good recommendations or your club might choose to read a book you otherwise would not have come across. Whether you are a fan of fiction or nonfiction, you can explore new genres and authors, gain insights on topics you were not previously familiar with, and maybe find some new favourites! You’ll also be more equipped to give books as gifts to friends and family, as you’ve become a more cultured reader.

**Book clubs are not just about books**. Even though the framework for book clubs is to talk about the book we’ve read or books in general, sometimes the discussion can stray away to other topics. All in all, no matter what your opinion on the book in question is, a book club is a great way to make and keep friends.

If you find reading books is a challenge for you, join a physical book club, opt for audio books or join a service like [onedown.club](https://onedown.club/) to encourage you to keep up your healthy habit. Try a one-book-a-month challenge and see how much better and smarter you’ll feel after a month or two.
