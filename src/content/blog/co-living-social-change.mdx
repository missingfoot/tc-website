Every idea starts with a problem. Ours was simple: there wasn’t a place where young people could bring new ideas that would help solve our city’s biggest challenges. Today we’re seeing issues that put a strain on our neighbourhoods that we’ve never seen before: new migrants living in poverty; little access to mental health support; families trapped in a cycle of economic downturn and so many more.

Originally we built co-living spaces to offer a more convenient and experience-led way of living, but we soon realised that co-living had the potential to be about more than just housing. It’s about fostering a community, and as such, a catalyst to drive social change.

But how exactly do you “drive social change”? It’s a tricky question to answer. Often people think you need to be an academic expert or have major financial backing, however, all it takes is young, forward-thinkers with big ideas to join forces and think outside the box. And that’s exactly what The Collective Foundation facilitates.

The Collective Foundation provides exemplary resources, well-designed programmes that support young entrepreneurs, and engages directly with its members. By providing all this it’s able to nurture ordinary citizens and enable them to have an extraordinary impact on their cities.

> We believe that being part of a community should be empowering. It should be a place where you can find fellow change-makers that enhance your ideas, launch them and make them stick. We also believe that absolutely everyone has the potential to change things for the better.

In August last year we invited nine social innovators from eight different countries that included Ghana, Colombia, USA and Malaysia, to be part of our [Global Accelerator](http://www.collectiveglobalaccelerator.com/) programme. The cohort of nine were chosen out of thousands of entrepreneurs who applied for a place. In that month, the cohort stayed at The Collective Old Oak where they got to know our members and live and work together to propel their ideas forward.

Our goal was to accelerate the impact that the entrepreneurs were already making within their communities. Some of the standouts include [Daniela](https://www.thebiznation.com/) who is revolutionising business education for young people in Latin America; [Elijah](http://foodforallafrica.org/) who uses food waste to save lives in West-Africa; Tizzita who is helping mothers in Ethiopia in their pregnancy journey and [Jaryd](http://www.muzitech.com/) who is working on ground-breaking technology to make HIV testing better. All of them are testaments to the positive change that one individual has the potential to make.

Learning from some of London’s brightest entrepreneurs and industry leaders through workshops, personal mentoring and networking sessions was a priceless experience for the cohort, as was learning from each other and the different challenges each of them faced.

At the heart of The Collective Global Accelerator is the idea of bringing talented people together to live and work under one roof – many of the Accelerator cohort felt that having the opportunity to co-live with their peers impacted their journey so deeply they described it as life-changing. Pieter, co-founder of Ava, which enables deaf and hard-of-hearing people to keep track of conversations around them, said that: “My peers have given me invaluable insights and being given the opportunity to live and work together has truly shifted my perspective on entrepreneurship, and life.”

That is what The Collective Foundation is about; creating opportunities that empower people to implement meaningful social change.
