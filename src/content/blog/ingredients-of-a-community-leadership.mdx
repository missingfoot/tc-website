“*Often, the leadership skills and qualities of these influential individuals are the ones that we end up identifying the most with, and the ones we try to bring forward in our own lives*.”

Right now at The Collective Old Oak, six fellows are taking part in our first ever [Community Facilitator Programme](/blog/a-week-in-the-life-of-our-first-community-fellows). That means they’re on the ground for the coming weeks, supporting our members in making their community amazing, while exploring tools to foster a brilliant community experience. Here, they continue to share their learnings.

As our peer to peer learning journey continues, we entered our Leadership Module through embodied learning practices. Our Leadership Guardians, Alisa, Ashen, and Laura, took the fellows through exercises where we learned by doing, practicing the Embodiment of Leadership through different experiences that were meant to take us through moments where we learned how to lead ourselves, but almost even more importantly, how to be led.

<Figure src="/images/blog/ingredients-of-a-community-leadership/01-the-collective-co-living-west-london-community.webp" alt="the collective co-living west london community" caption="The Fellows engaging in Leadership" />

One of our first exercises was to identify an inspirational leader in our lives, not necessarily a hero or idol but someone in our personal lives who we feel has leadership qualities and who was able to transfer that knowledge into our own selves. We then had to think of different attributes and characteristics this inspirational leader has or had, and relate those attributes to our own personalities.

**Often, the leadership skills and qualities of these influential individuals are the ones that we end up identifying the most with, and the ones we try to bring forward in our own lives**<em>.</em>

Two other practices that we experienced during this module were the ‘Angel Dance’ and the ‘Pair Walk’. These experiences required us to trust our partners to lead us through a dance or a tour through Old Oak while having our eyes closed. This practice of non-verbal communication forced us to put all our trust in our partner while they led us through the Old Oak building, and then we were responsible for switching roles and leading a tour with our partners afterwards. Both were practices in letting go, as we surrendered our own movements and gave the Leadership Guardians our trust, curiosity and non-judgement while they guided us through unexpected experiences.

<Figure src="/images/blog/ingredients-of-a-community-leadership/02-the-collective-old-oak-co-living-west-london-commu.webp" alt="the collective old oak co-living west london community" caption="The Four Fold Practice" />

The aim of these two experiences was to learn how to Lead and Be Lead, similar to the idea of Being Hosted and Hosting Others. In order to Be Hosted, one must practice their own Listening capacities, keep a Genuine Curiosity, Participate and become a skilful Learner. Hosting Others requires one to keep in mind different Types of Leadership (i.e. authoritarian/paternal vs. co-creation), practice [Emotional Storytelling](http://eu.wiley.com/WileyCDA/WileyTitle/productCd-0470548673.html) and learn how to Host Conversations. [The Art of Hosting](http://www.artofhosting.org/) (And Harvesting Conversations That Matter) is a community of practitioners who have developed a [Four-Fold Practice](https://vimeo.com/69785461) — Host Yourself, Be Hosted, Host Others, Be Part of a Community Hosting Itself — which involves:

> “<strong><em>Being truly present, engaging skilfully in conversations, being a good host of conversations and engaging with others in co-creation,</em></strong>”

in order to harvest collective intelligence for community participation. This has been a resource for our fellowship and the exercises we were taken through during the Leadership Module helped us embody this Four-Fold Practice.

We also did a few experiences where we had to sense the actions and feelings of our partners and the group as a whole, in order to achieve certain shared responsibilities or to respond and understand the feelings of our partner. Sensing the feelings and emotions of our partner and of the group facilitates the interactions and communication between us led to more creativity and co-creation. These group thinking exercises also tie into the concept of [Chaordic Systems Thinking](http://www.chaosforum.com/docs/nieuws/NOSMO%20CMT.pdf), which is “an approach to designing a complex organisational system that recognises the enterprise not as a fixed structure, but as ‘flow’. This systems thinking offers “new concepts in order to deal with uncontrollability, uncertainty and complexity” in an enterprise, organisation or community. Being a Systems Leader means being able to work with change, finding ways of creating contexts where people take ownership of ideas, creating conditions for collective learning and being able to switch between linear and circular ways of thinking (i.e. cause and effect vs. patterns thinking).

Regardless of the practice or technique you are adopting for your leadership skills, being a leader is about learning your own potentials for inspiring others to empower themselves. In Simon Sinek’s [*Leaders Eat Last*](https://www.amazon.co.uk/Leaders-Eat-Last-Together-Others/dp/0670923168)<em>: Why Some Teams Pull Together and Others Don’t,</em> Sinek explains: “<strong><em>If your actions inspire others to dream more, learn more, do more and become more, you are a leader.</em></strong>”

When an Authentic Leader remains grounded, present, well and selfless, they are able to create an environment where individuals can co-create great ideas through collective wisdom and learning. Co-living spaces like [The Collective Old Oak](https://www.thecollective.co.uk/coliving/old-oak) can be great places to inspire leadership skills within individuals of the community, giving them the confidence to design participatory experiences that have a positive social impact within their communities and outwards.
