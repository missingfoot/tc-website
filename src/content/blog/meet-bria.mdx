The Collective Old Oak is home to an amazing and diverse community. It brings together more than 500 people from around the world, from all walks of life. Together they create a rich network of perspectives and experiences. Continuing our series [*We Are The Collective*](/blog/we-are-the-collective), our community members tell us their stories.

This week, we caught up with our member Bria, a creative entrepreneurial spirit with a background in multimedia journalism.

<Figure src="/images/blog/meet-bria/01-the-collective-co-living-west-london-community-liv.webp" alt="the collective co-living west london community living" caption="Social butterfly and relationship builder." />

<strong>What makes you happy?</strong>

“People make me happy. I’m a very people-oriented person. Usually when I walk into a room full of strangers, I view it as I’m walking into a room full of potential friends.

When I first moved to The Collective I thought: ‘Okay, by the end of my first year, I want to have met 100 people in this building.’ My mum was like: ‘That’s very humble Bria, I’m sure you’ll meet well over 100!’ Just because she knows I’m very social.

I love building relationships intentionally. I love hanging out with people, getting to know people and understanding why they are the way they are. Just learning, really. I feel like people are just walking libraries of information and experience and I just — I love it.”

<strong>What motivates you?</strong>

“I get motivated by purpose. I think it’s hard for me to agree to something if I don’t love it. At the same time if I am committed to something, it means I’m absolutely 100% in. I’m committed physically, mentally, spiritually and just all in; I don’t do things halfway. I don’t believe in having one foot in, one foot out because I think you’ll only see results if you try to give it your all. So I think I’m motivated by just wanting to give it my all.

> <strong><em>There’s something magical about working in a team. It’s like a quilt, it’s like a tapestry. Every thread maybe has a different colour or a different texture but it all comes together to make this beautiful tapestry.</em></strong>

I’m motivated by achievement, I’m motivated by goals, I’m motivated by collaboration. Again, I’m very people-oriented, so I’m motivated by the opportunity to work with people and create something bigger than ourselves. I love being part of a team — not that I can’t work alone, but there’s something magical about working in a team. It’s like a quilt, it’s like a tapestry. Every thread maybe has a different colour or a different texture but it all comes together to make this beautiful tapestry. That is the feeling I get when I’m working with a team and we’re working towards a common goal. That’s something I find incredibly motivating.

I think in terms of motivation, especially for young adulthood, it’s key to ask yourself: ‘What am I doing?’ and ‘Where am I going?’ Because when you feel like you can answer either or both of those questions, there’s a click in your head and your heart, and it’s incredibly deep and exciting. It’s exciting to wake up and say: ‘This is what I’m going to do today. This is what I’m working towards.’

I struggled with that right after graduation, just asking myself: ‘Okay, what’s next? Where am I going? Oh my god, what am I meant to be doing? These are the skills I have learned, but what does it amount to?’

Sometimes it’s just about taking *a* *step* in a direction, and then things can start to make more sense. You can say: ‘I don’t like this’ or ‘I don’t like that,’ ‘I’m committed to this but I’m not committed to that,’ and you start to realise what you really stand for, and what really matters to you.”

<strong>Which aspect of your life are you most proud of?</strong>

“I’d say I am proud of my relationships. I have what I think and I have been told is an admirable relationship with my parents, especially with my mother. She’s really like a compass in my life. She always pulls me back to true north and she really has my best interests in mind. Aside from being a parental guide, she’s truly my best friend. I can tell her anything and everything. I know that a lot of people would say: ‘Oh yeah, but she’s your mother.’ But I cherish, dearly, the relationship I have with my mother, and also the relationships I have outside of my family.

I’m very big on intentional relationship building and I take great care to nurture my friendships and my relationships. When you’re engaged with people, it’s an experience. And so every time I’m with my friends or meeting new friends, it’s an experience. You get out of an experience what you put into it. And so whatever I put into a new relationship, I’ll get out. So if I put in love and fun and excitement and encouragement and support, then I’ll probably get those things out as well. It’s like a nice, little healthy loop.

So I’m very proud of the people I have in my circle of influence, many of whom are also just achieving their dreams and have found their purpose and are very motivated at this time in their lives. That kind of energy is contagious and I feel like it rubs off on me and makes me want to be with them.

I’m also proud of the fact that I managed to achieve a dream of mine — to study and live in London, and I did just that.”

Bria said: “I’m proud of the fact that I managed to achieve a dream of mine to study and live in London.”

<strong>Is it as fulfilling as you thought it would be?</strong>

“Even beyond, way beyond. London has layers, and the longer you’re living here, the deeper you can dig into the experience of living here. I’ve found I’ve kind of gotten lost, but in a good way. And I think in doing so, I’ve found myself and my purpose. I’m really proud of that. It’s so crazy to think that two years ago it was just a dream, a hope. Now I can actually say that I’ve done it.”
