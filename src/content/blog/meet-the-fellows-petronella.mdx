Right now at The Collective Old Oak, six fellows are taking part in our first ever Community Facilitator Programme. That means they’re on the ground for the next few months, supporting our members in making their home amazing. Along the way, they’re on a peer-led journey exploring tools to foster a brilliant community experience.

We’re spotlighting each fellow in a series of profiles. Today, meet Petronella!

<Figure src="/images/blog/meet-the-fellows-petronella/cover.webp" alt="the collective co-living london community" caption="“I like to bring people together and witness the spark happen.”" />

Understanding the complexities of community cohesion has been a theme throughout Petronella’s work, study and lifestyle.

Growing up in a rural area, she realised the interdependencies of village life, family and friendships. She has since studied and worked overseas, casting a global web through her work — which has seen her involved in community planning around climate change, keeping our independent high streets thriving with The Big Issue, and working with hubs that nourish social impact. The past two years meanwhile have seen Petro living and working in rural North Somerset, focussing her efforts on community resilience and self reliance, understanding the rural economy and challenging perspectives on rural life.

On The Collective’s Fellowship programme, Petro said: “I love the ambition of nourishing 500 odd members with practices that encourage mutual respect, self respect and care for one another.”

She told us more about herself by finishing some sentences:

<strong><em>To me, community means…</em></strong>

Interdependence, recognition, safety and a deep comfort in my belly that someone has my back.

<strong><em>It’s important to connect with people because…</em></strong>

It is our lifeline. Without those bonds, without acknowledgement from our neighbour that we exist, it can lead to much turmoil! It is important too, to bond with people outside of our own perceived demographic or comfort zone to represent true community — where values may be shared but explored in different ways. To illustrate, we may all decide we all value ‘health’ — some people will go to the gym, some people will eat loads of vegetables, some people will meditate, some people will eat superfood powders…it is all demonstrated in different ways, and that is what makes true community sparkle, the energy we get from diversity, the ‘mezcla’ (a favourite Spanish word that means the mixture and blend).

For, [<em>“</em>in ecosystems it is at the edge, between a forest and a meadow for example, where there is the highest biodiversity and most number of relationships between different species. As a result it is there that evolution is the most powerful, where change and innovation are boosted.”](https://labofii.wordpress.com/2015/08/23/drawing-a-line-in-the-sand-the-movement-victory-at-ende-gelande-opens-up-the-road-of-disobedience-for-paris/)

And that is what we create at The Collective!

<strong><em>My biggest fear is…</em></strong>

That we forget how to be with people we haven’t met before, that we will hide ourselves away to prefer the ease of online versions of ourselves and fear being truly ‘seen’.

<strong><em>I am happiest when…</em></strong>

When I watch connections happen! When I see someone reach out to someone else and the energy is mutual.

<em>People have described me as a ‘fixer,’ which to me sounds like something from the mafia, but I like to bring people together and witness the spark happen.</em>

<strong><em>In the future, I hope…</em></strong>

I am no longer needed, that the job is redundant — that community is recognised as a human need, to belong and to exist offline as well as online and thus humanity prioritises it as their responsibility to nurture…And that communities also recognise their shared responsibility, and how they can leverage change in a world that seeks our wisdom and care from living well at the community level to expand to the global scale.
